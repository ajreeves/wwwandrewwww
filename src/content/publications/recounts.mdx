## Research Question

How closely do initial vote counts agree with recounts, and do optically scanned paper ballots produce smaller discrepancies than hand-counted ballots?

## Main Finding

In the 2000–2004 comparison, the weighted average discrepancy was 1.72% for hand-counted ballots and 0.68% for machine-counted ballots after excluding the exceptional Dover and Bradford cases (Table 12.1, p. 169). Regression estimates associate optical scanning with a discrepancy about 0.8 percentage points lower than hand counting (Table 12.2, p. 170).

## Research Design

The chapter compares initial counts with recounts, using 1946–1962 records as a historical benchmark and 2000, 2002, and 2004 records to compare hand counting with optical scanning. Regression analyses account for the total vote, the candidate's initial vote, and the office at stake.

## Data Employed

The historical analysis uses 415 candidate-by-jurisdiction tabulations from 108 recounted races in 1946–1962. The later analysis uses 1,331 tabulations from 60 recounted races in 2000, 2002, and 2004 (pp. 167–168). Although the chapter title ends in 2002, the published analysis includes 2004.

## Substantive Importance

Recounts can reveal discrepancies that matter in close elections. The comparison supports optical scanning in this setting, while emphasizing that tabulation accuracy is only one part of election accuracy: the measure does not capture every problem in marking, handling, or interpreting ballots.
